⚠️ Spoilers for all of episode 7 of *Doctor X: Mafia in White* (including the ending), the official episode 8 preview, and episode 7 of season 1 of the Japanese original. Interpretations are labeled in the text as *pure speculation* or *my take*.

Late in episode 7, after the surgery, Director Bu Seung-gwon tells Gye Su-jeong that he allowed the operation on the premise that it was a bullet. Gye Su-jeong's answer is short. "I never said it was a bullet."

After episode 6 last week, honestly, I was worried. There were so many comments like ‘it peaked at episode 5’ and ‘I’m dropping it here.’ But in episode 7, Gye Su-jeong tells Bu Seung-gwon she has no interest in building or moving the world. Yes, the Gye Su-jeong I knew is back.

So today we'll unpack two things.

First: in last week's preview, Bu Seung-gwon talks about a lie Gye Su-jeong told. What on earth was it?

Second: the words Dr. Woo remembers Oh Sang-beom crying out when he woke from sleep. "It's too heavy. Heavy." What was the weight that crushed Oh Sang-beom for 46 years?

## The transfer

After the night Gye Su-jeong's team caught Oh Sang-beom on the roof, Ms. Jang sets out a meal in the hospital lounge. Ms. Jang tells Team Doctor X, gathered around the table, that the hospital meeting brought up a transfer. They want to send Oh Sang-beom to another hospital. And in the hospital's VIP ward lies former President Ji Du-seok, who was brought in during episode 6.

Park Tae-gyeong flares up. Why should Oh Sang-beom be transferred, he says, the one who should go is that man. Dr. Woo, who has stayed by Oh Sang-beom's side all this time, asked Ms. Jang to let her take him back down. She says the surgery is something that shouldn't be done. The hospital wants him out, and his guardian wants to take him away. Gye Su-jeong's surgery looks set to end before it even begins.

Protesters outside the hospital, and Oh Sang-beom screaming every night inside. Park Tae-gyeong, who watches over him, mutters that he can't sleep and can't stay awake like this. There is nowhere in this hospital where Oh Sang-beom can rest.

## "You're doing it to die, right?"

That night, though, Gye Su-jeong sits down in front of Oh Sang-beom and asks. "You're doing it to die, right?" He wasn't trying to live. He was trying to get the surgery in order to die. Gye Su-jeong brings up what she said in episode 6: if it fails, he dies. What Oh Sang-beom wanted from the surgery was exactly that failure.

But Gye Su-jeong's answer is that she can't do it. She won't let him run away from the possibility that he's alive. She can't fail for him. At that, Dr. Woo breaks down. You have to live well, she says, you have to try living.

How did Gye Su-jeong know, though? Park Tae-gyeong wondered too. How did she know he's a man who lives in order to die? Gye Su-jeong's answer was that she knew the look. The look of someone who punishes themselves simply by being alive.

So where had Gye Su-jeong seen that look? There's an easy-to-miss scene early in episode 7. Watching Oh Sang-beom struggle all night, Gye Su-jeong says to Ms. Jang that this is how it felt. When Ms. Jang asks what she means, she says: back then. Gye Su-jeong waking from nightmares and wailing, and the people beside her telling her it's all right, you're home. Ms. Jang and the people of the agency, the very people Gye Su-jeong now lives with like family.

Right in the middle of episode 7, a worn-out Ms. Jang says let's let it go. She says the thought came to her the moment she was holding Oh Sang-beom on the roof. Even if the surgery goes well, could you say that man will have a better life? How do you save someone who throws himself at death? Gye Su-jeong throws it back: you've saved someone before, haven't you? And one more line. "You saved a life."

From here on it's Gordon Insight pure speculation. The person Ms. Jang saved might be Gye Su-jeong herself. The Gye Su-jeong of back then, for whom being alive was itself a punishment. That's why she recognized that look at once.

And then Park Tae-gyeong tells Gye Su-jeong that he just wants that man to get some sleep. Remember that line. It comes back after the surgery.

## Reenacting that night

Oh Sang-beom was injured in the head 46 years ago, at the age of twelve. What went into his head that night is the key to episode 7.

Midway through episode 7, with Oh Sang-beom's screams behind her, Gye Su-jeong mutters to herself that she has to think, she has to find it. Then Ms. Jang passes on a story from 1980. When soldiers tried to take Oh Sang-beom away, a hospital doctor told them there was a bullet lodged in his head. One word caught on me here. Not "a bullet was lodged," but "he said a bullet was lodged."

At Ms. Jang's request, people gather in one place. Prof. Kim Seong-jin reenacts that night 46 years ago as a play, in the space most like that place. At the moment the gunfire pours down, the older brother covers his younger brother with his whole body. But Prof. Kim Seong-jin doesn't stop there. One brother alone wouldn't have been enough, he says. The restaurant owner, the brother's friend, and then the uncle each cover the child, one after another.

Gye Su-jeong keeps saying she couldn't understand it. How did a twelve-year-old survive that? In the room after the play, Gye Su-jeong tells Oh Sang-beom that maybe it wasn't only his brother's body lying on that child. At that, Oh Sang-beom opens his mouth. "It was heavy." And then Oh Sang-beom shaves his head. He has decided to have the surgery.

Now let's go back for a moment to the opening's second question. Half of the weight Oh Sang-beom spoke of is explained here. It was the bodies of the people who covered him. The other half comes out in the operating room.

## Träumerei

Before the surgery, what Gye Su-jeong says to Oh Sang-beom is that you only die once. So save it. Put it off until tomorrow, and the day after, again and again. She promises to take him home together with his brother. And then this line comes back. "I don't ever fail." This is the line I was waiting for. The one that carried us through episodes 1 to 5.

The surgery Gye Su-jeong chose doesn't open the head wide. She goes in with an endoscope and takes out the foreign object. She goes deep into the head of a patient in his fifties by the smallest possible path.

Schumann's *Träumerei* plays in the operating room. It's German for "dreaming." In the operating room of a man who couldn't sleep for 46 years because of a dream, a piece called dreaming plays. In the anesthetized Oh Sang-beom's dream are the songs he sang with his brother, the arcade, and his brother's last wish that he eat well, sleep well, and live like that. Why Gye Su-jeong promised to send him home together with his brother, the real meaning of that line comes at the very end.

And the moment Gye Su-jeong asks for the biopsy forceps, Park Tae-gyeong quietly leaves the operating room, as he was instructed in advance. Why did she send Park Tae-gyeong out? The reason comes right after the surgery ends.

## The specimen container

The observation room is in an uproar. A bullet is what everyone was waiting for. When the surgery ends, everyone blocks Gye Su-jeong's way, but the name she calls is Park Tae-gyeong. Park Tae-gyeong catches the specimen container Gye Su-jeong tosses and runs straight off, and the head of the doctors' association and the mayor's people chase after him.

But in the hallway everyone ran down after Park Tae-gyeong, one person stayed behind. Gye Su-jeong's line: "I knew you'd stay behind, Director."

The conversation at the very start of this post came right after that. Bu Seung-gwon points at Gye Su-jeong's pocket and asks whether the thing in the other specimen container is what he thinks it is. When she asks what he means, Bu Seung-gwon answers: a bone fragment. At that moment I thought, so letting everyone call it a bullet throughout episode 6 was all calculated.

Last week's preview, where Bu Seung-gwon said everyone would soon learn about the lie Dr. Gye had told, was this very scene. The answer to the lie everyone had wondered about since episode 6 was in a small container in Gye Su-jeong's pocket.

So what was in the container Park Tae-gyeong ran with? When they opened it, it was an empty tube. But according to Park Tae-gyeong it was full: with Dr. Gye's heart for her patient. This is where I burst out laughing. The reason Gye Su-jeong sent Park Tae-gyeong out was a decoy. But the real reason Park Tae-gyeong was so thrilled was something else. Earlier, Gye Su-jeong had called his name for the first time.

That night, beside the soundly sleeping Oh Sang-beom in his hospital room, Gye Su-jeong talks to herself. Looks like he isn't dreaming tonight. The sleep Park Tae-gyeong had wished for came to him for the first time in 46 years.

So why did Gye Su-jeong never say it wasn't a bullet? She answers directly in front of Bu Seung-gwon. If those noisy people had known it was a bone fragment, would they have backed the surgery? It had to be a bullet to make a just-looking picture. As I see it, she saved the patient with the support of the people who needed that picture.

And then she adds. She's neither ‘yes’ nor ‘no’, just someone who asks ‘why’. A doctor with no interest in moving the world who only asks her patient why, and yet who reads the board and even prepares a decoy. That's the Gye Su-jeong we've been watching since episode 1.

## The white lie

At the news that it isn't a bullet, the mayor's side and the doctors' association are thrown into turmoil. But on the press conference stage, Mayor Im Ho-seok calls that bone fragment a shard of sorrow, lodged there when pain shattered. The people who needed a bullet yesterday are painting their picture with a bone fragment today.

Meanwhile, Ms. Jang turns down what the hospital offered and insists on one thing: that the support for the surgery not be cancelled. She says she refuses so no one can call her shameless. In episode 6 it looked like Ms. Jang would finally replace her car, and now she mutters that she wanted to do it this time.

At the press conference, Director Bu Seung-gwon takes the microphone and gets a sharp question. Surely the test results alone would have shown it wasn't a bullet, so why did he operate? Bu Seung-gwon answers that he judged it a bone fragment, not a bullet, and then goes back 46 years. When martial-law troops tried to take twelve-year-old Oh Sang-beom away, the doctor at the time probably told them a bullet was lodged in his head. Bu Seung-gwon calls that a ‘white lie’.

By Bu Seung-gwon's account, the doctor's lie 46 years ago kept the soldiers from taking the child, and Gye Su-jeong's lie today protected the surgery. Bu Seung-gwon called the two by the same name. In the early part of episode 7 the hospital was trying to send Oh Sang-beom to another hospital, yet in front of the reporters Bu Seung-gwon says a transfer was never something he considered.

And before Bu Seung-gwon's last words are finished, a Code Blue sounds. VIP ward, room 5: Ji Du-seok. Bu Seung-gwon steps down from the stage without finishing his sentence, and the TV runs a breaking news report that former President Ji Du-seok has died at the age of 91.

A word about the original, too. The title of episode 7 of the Japanese original's season 1 is "Isn't it a doctor's duty to tell the truth?" Episode 7 of the original is also about persuading a patient who refuses surgery. But where the original says to tell the truth, the Korean version's Gye Su-jeong protected the surgery with a lie. To my eyes, the same episode 7 arrived at opposite answers. By the way, the line "I don't ever fail" comes from the original too. It's Daimon Michiko's signature line, *Watashi, shippai shinai node* (私、失敗しないので), roughly "I don't fail, you see."

## The karaoke room

Meanwhile, Oh Sang-beom, who had disappeared from his hospital room. Park Tae-gyeong found him at a karaoke room. Oh Sang-beom went in carrying a black plastic bag from a convenience store. That black bag worries Park Tae-gyeong. Park Tae-gyeong picks up the microphone and sings the song Oh Sang-beom used to sing with his brother in his dream.

But back when the press conference was being held, Gye Su-jeong had said something odd to Jo Won-jin. That the bone fragment wasn't the patient Oh Sang-beom's. If the fragment that came out of Oh Sang-beom's head isn't Oh Sang-beom's, whose is it? The clue was in Ms. Jang's phone call. The one where she said the results would be in soon, and that if she was wrong, there'd be no meals for two days.

As the song fades, Gye Su-jeong sits beside Oh Sang-beom. She says she hadn't told him earlier because she was waiting for the DNA test result, and what she gives him is this. "It's your brother's bone fragment." To Oh Sang-beom, who called for his brother asleep and awake, Gye Su-jeong adds that he was with him the whole time.

## Episode 8 preview

Then, in the last minute, the mood shifts sharply. The news continues, and another breaking report: the government has announced an increase in the medical school admission quota. Adding more than 2,000 seats starting next year. The doctors' association and the trainee doctors immediately announce a strike.

Feels like a scene you've seen before. In February 2024, the government announced a 2,000-seat increase in the medical school quota, and trainee doctors left their hospitals. A year and a half later: the 2025 admission figure was settled not at 2,000 more but at 4,567, an increase of 1,509. In April 2025 the government returned the 2026 admission figure to the pre-increase 3,058. The trainee doctors who left their hospitals came back in the second-half recruitment in September 2025, 7,984 of them. That was 76% of the level before the dispute.

But this February, the government again decided to raise the quota. 490 seats for the 2027 admission year, around 3,300 over five years. The doctors' association objected, but unlike two years ago, it didn't go to mass resignation. The scale and the method are different, but honestly, I found it a bit odd. Back then it was a big enough deal that doctors left their hospitals. Why is it so quiet now?

In the episode 8 preview, as trainee doctors stop working and leave the hospital, a ten-year-old child patient appears. In the middle of the medical vacuum, Gye Su-jeong prepares that child's surgery. The doctors split among themselves. Words like ‘traitor’ are thrown at the ones who say they'll stay. And at the end, Gye Su-jeong's line. "If you're a doctor, get to the surgery."

Gye Su-jeong, who said she had no interest in moving the world. In episode 8, that world knocks on the door of her operating room.

## Two answers

Let me wrap up today's two questions.

Gye Su-jeong's lie was letting people believe a bone fragment was a bullet. That was the only way to protect the surgery.

And the weight Oh Sang-beom spoke of, as I see it, was the bodies of the people who covered him. And his brother, who had been with him in his head for 46 years.
